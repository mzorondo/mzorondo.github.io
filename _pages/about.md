I am an economist researching how a **Universal Basic Income (UBI)** could reshape entrepreneurship, innovation, and the future of work. I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Innovation and Management at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, where I build **agent-based computational economic (ACE) models** — extending the Keynes+Schumpeter (K+S) macroeconomic modeling tradition — to study how income guarantees change firm entry, labor markets, and long-run growth.

My current work develops an entrepreneurial-entry extension to the K+S framework, modeling the conditions under which workers become entrepreneurs and how a UBI shifts that calculus — with a particular interest in *necessity entrepreneurship*, market dynamism, and economic resilience.

Before economics, I spent a decade in experimental plasma physics, characterizing dense plasma focus devices and modeling Thomson scattering from fusion plasmas. That background shapes how I approach economics today: as a complex, dynamic system best understood through simulation, data, and careful measurement rather than equilibrium shortcuts. I feel a deep sense of political responsibility in this work, which is why I moved from physics toward researching and promoting UBI.
