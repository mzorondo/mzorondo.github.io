I am an interdisciplinary researcher studying, from a complex evolutionary economic approach, how a **Unconditional Basic Income (UBI)** could reshape entrepreneurship, innovation, and the future of work. I am a Ph.D. candidate in Innovation and Management at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, where I am building an extension to the **Keynes meets Schumpeter (K+S) Agent-Based models (ABM)** to study how a UBI changes labor bargaining power, firm entry, and long-run growth.

Currently, I am developing an entrepreneurial-entry extension to the K+S framework, modeling the conditions under which workers become entrepreneurs. In parallel, I am introducing a UBI to the model to study both its effect on the macroeconomy and its interaction with entrepreneurial entry, with a particular interest in *opportunity/necessity entrepreneurship*, market dynamism, labor bargaining power, and market competition. 

I began my research career in physics, with a decade of work going from complexity science to experimental physics. I did research on complex networks and evolutionary automatons, then I progressively moved through optical, electrical, nuclear, and plasma experimental work, combined with plasma theory, in an effort to bridge theoretical and experimental work. This background shapes how I approach economics today: as a complex, dynamic system best understood through the combination of simulation, theory, and experiments.
